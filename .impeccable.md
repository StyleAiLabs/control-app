# Sync360 Design Context

## Users

**Primary:** Non-technical business owners (tradies, plumbers, service operators) who want something that "just works" — they need confidence and clarity without feeling overwhelmed.

**Secondary:** Office managers/dispatchers who live in the system daily — they need efficiency, clear status, and fast access to what matters.

**Context of use:** Quick check-ins from workshops, on-site visits, or vehicles. Potentially bright environments. Mobile and tablet use likely. Users are focused on getting work done, not learning software.

**Emotional goals:** Calm confidence, clarity, trust. The interface should feel like a competent team member — supportive, not demanding attention.

## Brand Personality

**Voice:** Warm, supportive, human — like having an extra team member who cares about the business.

**Tone:** Approachable but professional. Straightforward without being cold. Encouraging without being cheerleader-ish.

**Three words:** Warm, reliable, human.

## Aesthetic Direction

**Visual tone:** Clean and professional with warmth baked in. The orange accent (#FF6B35) is a good anchor — it's energetic but not aggressive. Lean into the "human" quality through:

- Generous whitespace that breathes
- Clear visual hierarchy that guides without shouting
- Subtle warmth in neutrals (avoiding cold grays)
- Purposeful decoration only — no filler

**Theme:** Support both light and dark modes. Light for daytime/on-site use (better in bright environments). Dark for evening admin work or users who prefer it.

**Avoid:** Corporate/sterile enterprise software aesthetics. Cold blues, harsh contrasts, dense information density, impersonal iconography.

## Design Principles

1. **Calm competence** — The interface should feel like a capable team member who has everything under control. No visual noise, no panic, no clutter.

2. **Human warmth** — This isn't enterprise software for faceless corporations. Every touchpoint should feel like it was made for a specific person running their business.

3. **Clarity over cleverness** — Business owners using this are focused on their work, not admiring UI tricks. Make information obvious, actions clear, and status unambiguous.

4. **Progressive disclosure** — Start simple, reveal sophistication through interaction. The dashboard should be scannable in 5 seconds but reward deeper engagement.

5. **Respect the context** — Users might be on a dusty job site, in a noisy office, or checking messages at 9pm from the couch. Design for interrupted attention and environmental distraction.
